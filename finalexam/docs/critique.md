Critique on 2024-2025 Campus Travel Survey
# Data Biography
The purpose of this survey and data analysis was to figure out the UC Davis
Sustainability practices and to understand how people affiliated with UC Davis
traveled to campus during a single week in Fall Quarter of 2024. The data was 
generated from an online survey through Qualtrics. Some key variables included
role_primary (level of surveyer ) and mode_all (mode of transportation). 
The data was provided through a CSV file. There were a lot of values like -99 
which where filtered out or NA. The data analysis was then conducted using 
Gemini 3.1 pro LLM.

# Critique
The survey results stated that 23.1% respondents who used a bike on campus
experienced theft or vandalism and that 8.9% had their entire bike stolen while
11.0% reported stolen bike parts. After conducting EDA and filtering out NA values,
I can confirm that out of the dataset 8.9% did have their bike stolen, and 10.97% 
had bike parts stolen and 23.14% had experienced one or the other. 76.86% did not
report an experience where their bike was stolen. We can see in that in Figure 1.
Figure 1 shows us a bar plot on bike theft or vandalism. We first removed any missing values
and then went and counted the number for each act and found the percentage. As the figure shows,
it tells the same story as the survey results where theft does reamin a significant deterrent to 
people biking on campus.

Largest respondent groups are Seniors as we can see in Figure 3 and we can also
see that responodents are primarily female as well in Figure 2. These figures agree
with the Campus Demographic survey generated by Gemini 3.1. Figure 2 shows that 
majority of the respondents were women. This could mean that for the Gender Comfort Gap
mentioned in the survey results that Men are twice as likely to report feeling Comfortable biking 
on campus compared to women could be skewed a bit as more women were surveyed than men.
Even though more women reported that they strongly agree with being felt safe while riding on campus,
men have a higher percentage of feeling safe compared to other men as we can see in
Figure 4. Overall, figure 2, 3, and 4 reinforce that men do feel more comfortable while riding
a bike on campus. We can see in figure 4 that men have a higher percent for those who strongly 
agree with feeling safe while riding on campus.

Figure 5 shows the amount of respondents who have financial pressure. It confirms the finding that
961 respondents cited that rent and mortgage was expensive. This makes it so financial and housing
are also reasons why students may not be using certain modes of transportation.

Figure 6 shows uncertainty on why people may not take public transport. We can see some of the uncertainty
when it comes to the bus arriving to campus on time.

There are also a lot of missing data values within the dataset as well as a lot of -99. The dataset
is also confusing sometimes when the answers are on a degree of somewhat agree or disagree.
This makes it hard to figure out if respondents are truly feeling like someone who fully agrees.
Some limtiations, this dataset has are than the majority of the survey respondents were women and
that there were a lot of questions that went unanswered which led to a lot of missing values. Overall,
these figures and visualizations support that key barriers to sustainable transit are lack of safety
and gendered differences in comfort on biking on campus. To improve this, designs should be focused
on these two main features.
