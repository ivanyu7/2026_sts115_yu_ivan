name is Average Transit Speeds by Route
2. I selected this dataset because it was a dataset I found interesting as 
I am interested in the public transit speeds and seeing how it could 
compare to driving yourself. I would like to see the data and see how much 
money you are saving taking public transit versus how much more time it 
may take if you do take public transit instead of driving yourself.
3. Metadata
Title: Average Transit Speeds by Route
Contributors: California Department of Transportation.
Assumptions: It is recorded in average speeds for routes.
Biases: Some biases include the agency reporting their times and also the 
routes the transit take depends on if someone could be late and slowing 
down the speed.
Ethical concerns: None
The data structure is in a CSV file. Some variables in this dataset 
include the route_id, time_period, speed_mph, direction_id, agency, 
route_name. 
4. Data Biography
The dataset contains route names, the average speed and different agencies 
for public transit. 
The dataset comes from the California Department of Transportation. It 
was collected the California Department of Transportation as well. It was 
collected to see the average speed of public transportation based on the 
routes. There aren't any clear privacy or legal concerns around this 
dataset. Some limitations could be the amount of time it is for certain 
days as the time period only describes if it's during the peak or not. 
This could lead to various days like the weekend where it could be 
shorter.
5. Some things I discovered that interested me was the time period is not 
separated by the day so it will be a bit harder to interpret as weekends 
may be faster than weekdays depending on the traffic. In addition, these 
speeds are estimated by GTFS which means the speed could be different than 
what was actually observed. This would make it harder to know whether or 
not these estimates are actually cose too the observed speed.
